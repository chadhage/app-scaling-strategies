S035 — Login callback during cutover

Start one login on v1, change routing, then deliver its callback to v2. Preserve correlation, the original return path and the original latency budget, without loops or forced reauthentication.

A-S035 · D-S035 · packet SC-S035 · one Kano S unit · standalone synthetic lesson

Worked exercise

  1. Keep the defaults and predict SUCCESS. Start login, cut over, then deliver the callback: v1 starts at 0 ms, routing changes at 40 ms, v2 receives the callback at 140 ms and completes at 160 ms.
  2. Choose v1-only state and predict FAILURE. v2 loses correlation and would require a forced restart. No replacement login is executed. The failed journey remains in history.
  3. Try the redirect-loop path: three synthetic hops stop with FAILURE, not unlimited retries. Try a 159 ms budget: even a completed login is late.
  4. Choose missing observations and predict UNKNOWN. A passing prediction does not prove a completed login.

Precondition: one synthetic learner and compatible versions, except for your selected fault. No real authentication, identity provider, token, key, credential or browser redirect is involved. All time is synthetic; there are no timers or network calls.

Fixture rules and limits

Cutover costs 40 ms. A compatible callback completes 20 ms after arrival. Each of at most three loop hops costs 20 ms. Budget equality passes; elapsed time greater than the original budget fails. Complete observations check v1-only state loss first, then marker mismatch, then loop behavior. Missing callback observations cannot prove any terminal outcome.
