S013 — Concurrency/queue-driven scale-out

A-S013 · D-S013 · SC-S013 · one frozen satisfier (S), kanoUnit A-S013

Objective: use supported workload metrics to increase capacity before excessive request accumulation. A rule trigger and requested capacity are not proof of ready workers or work finishing by its original deadline.

Declared synthetic fixture and thresholds

  • Start: two ready workers. Each ready worker completes up to two FIFO items per five-second window.
  • Select queue depth ≥ 8 items OR concurrency ≥ 6 requests per ready worker. Only the selected metric controls the rule. Concurrency is an independent, fixed pressure observation, not a counted work item.
  • A threshold breach requests one worker. One further window is needed for an observed ready transition; maximum four workers, no automatic scale-in or retries. Requested workers cannot serve work.
  • Each advance adds five synthetic seconds, observes readiness, samples pre-service pressure, evaluates the selected rule, then records bounded completions at that boundary. These discrete teaching steps are not a physical performance forecast.
  • Each batch keeps its submission time and original absolute deadline = submission + chosen budget. Completion at the deadline is ON_TIME; completion later is LATE. Unfinished work past its deadline is overdue.
  • Missing metrics/capacity/completions are UNKNOWN, never zero or success. Missing capacity pauses modeled service and warm-up; last-confirmed counts are historical. Provisioning and worker errors stay in the trace after recovery.
  • CPU percent is unsupported in this workload-rule fixture, not universally unsupported by Azure. Native metric/adapter/host eligibility is unverified. Unsupported or unqualified selections execute nothing.

Preconditions and limits: a modern browser with JavaScript; open this file directly. No server, packages, framework, telemetry, real queue, load generator, management calls, credentials, subscription, identity, cloud resources, deployment, or spend. Thresholds and service rates are explicit teaching constants, not Azure defaults. Node.js 22 is test tooling only; production Node.js 24 and native B-S013/D-S013 acceptance remain unvalidated.

Conduct the workload-driven transition

  1. Keep the defaults: queue metric, batch 12, concurrency 12, deadline 20 seconds. Enqueue one batch.
  2. Advance one window. Queue 12 triggers requested 3 / ready 2; four items finish at 5s and eight remain.
  3. Advance a second window. The third worker is observed ready; six more items finish at 10s.
  4. Advance a third window. All 12 finish by 15s, within their original 20s deadline. Inspect actual added-worker completions and the separate rule/capacity/work/verifier outputs.
  5. Archive/reset, choose concurrency, apply and repeat. 12 requests ÷ 2 ready workers = 6, so the inclusive threshold triggers.
  6. Archive/reset, set batch 40 / deadline 5, apply, enqueue and step twice. Scale-out still occurs, but late and overdue items make the verifier FAIL. Recover and inspect the retained misses.

Every action is explicit; nothing advances on a wall clock. Apply controls before enqueuing or stepping. Applying input changes future observations only; it cannot rewrite old deadlines or cancel a warming request.
