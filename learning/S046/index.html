S046 — Server-sent events: resume the cursor

Resume after the last rendered event, then prove order and completeness. A newly opened SSE stream is not evidence that the user missed nothing.

Source S046 · D-S046 · A-S046 / SC-S046 / one S unit

Worked fixture and operating envelope

Precondition: the old stream rendered evt-amber → evt-birch → evt-cedar. Its saved cursor is evt-cedar. The required suffix is evt-delta → evt-elm → evt-fir. These opaque IDs have the fixture's declared order, not a universal numeric SSE sequence.

  1. Choose a cursor, stream condition, replay policy and original budget before starting.
  2. Start the old stream, cut over, then resume with the synthetic Last-Event-ID request.
  3. Inspect the visible IDs, individual checks, timing and retained results. Try an expired replay window after a successful run.
  4. Predict success, failure or unknown. A correct prediction is separate from the user's outcome.

All times are simulated. Time zero is the last old visible event. Opening takes the chosen reconnect delay; each received wire event takes another 20 ms, including suppressed replays. Both silence and final recovery must fit the original budget; stream-open never restarts it. Exact equality passes.

No server, account, credentials, subscription or real EventSource is used. Six events, at most eight retained attempts, delays 0–2000 ms and budgets 1–2000 ms bound this exercise.

Limits and evidence level

This finite replay fixture does not establish a general SSE retention, transport, reconnect or exactly-once guarantee. It performs no network or Azure operations and measures no live latency. Deduplication here is scoped to the six fixed synthetic IDs.
