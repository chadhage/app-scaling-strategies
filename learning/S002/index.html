Prepared native slot swap

Warm the candidate for its destination. Keep the distinction between a retained slot and a retained worker.

Objective: choose a supported native swap configuration with production as target, and explain why former production work is not protected from recycling.

Before you start

This is a file-browser, SOURCE-only fixture: staging holds synthetic v2; production serves synthetic v1. Two staging instances, shared compatible state, and overlap headroom are assumed. No runtime framework is chosen, approved, installed, or hosted.

Native preparation applies applicable target slot-specific settings to the source and restarts it. Warm-up must be meaningful for dependencies and representative journeys. After routing changes, former production workers are restarted/recycled even if their slot is retained.

The fixture models three non-secret, slot-specific aliases: APP_ENV=production, CATALOG_ALIAS=catalog-production, SESSION_CONTRACT=shared-v1. It does not imply every setting swaps: identity, networking, authentication, and other effective settings require separate validation.

IaC: N/A. No resources, credentials, deployment, or management calls. C001 runtime/auth/hosting decisions and separate scaffold/deploy approvals are absent. A-S002 does not implement B-S002 or complete D-S002.

    Cleanup — local only

    Zero cloud resources created. No persistent local state, credentials, timer, service worker, or network activity is used.

    Reset clears selections, trace, acknowledgments, verifier, and impact in this page only. Closing the page also discards its in-memory state. There is no live action to undo or claim to reconcile.

    Limits you must carry forward

    • In-flight requests can be interrupted by former-worker recycling. Long-lived connections and background jobs need separate protection; native swap does not promise custom drain semantics.
    • Retaining the old slot, retrying, reconnecting, or rolling back cannot erase interruptions. Retries and reconnects remain inside the original journey budget.
    • Actual maximum request/job durations, native lifecycle timing, session/authentication continuity, correctness, and durable or exactly-once effects are unmeasured.
    • Slots share App Service plan compute. A swap adds no capacity and provides no independent fault domain. Overlap/dependency headroom here is an assumption.
    • HTTP status alone is not readiness; representative state, dependency, configuration, and journey checks are still required.
    • No live migration, native guarantee, uptime target, five-nines claim, or reliability certification follows from this lesson. Unverified stays unverified.
